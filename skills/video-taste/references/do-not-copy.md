# Do not copy

These are the signature moves of the five reference films. Each one is an idea that belonged to its film's product. Reusing one makes your film a cover version, and the critic's originality check will flag frames that come too close.

Learn the principle in the right-hand column. Then find the version that comes from your product.

| Reference move (off-limits) | Why it works (keep this) |
|---|---|
| A big numeral or letter built from dozens of archival materials, cut every 3 frames | One fixed shape lets very fast cuts read as one thought |
| A circular eyepiece or lens mask that dives into a montage of sciences (botany, maps, microscopy, stars) | The film's verb ("look closer") becomes the camera |
| Every shot is the curved edge of something (planet, pastry, leaf, petri dish), with one serif line locked above it | A repeated horizon line makes a montage feel like one continuous place |
| Butterflies flying into the lens as the transition to a colored title card whose color comes from the last shot | The title's color is earned from the imagery, not picked from a palette |
| Headlines typed letter by letter on a white canvas with one green accent word | Reading speed sets the pace; one accent says what matters |
| A prompt box typing a request while the camera pulls back from an extreme close-up of one control | Push in on the one thing that matters, then reveal the whole |
| App windows floating on a deep-space backdrop | A change of world marks the shift from "what it is" to "what it does" |
| Words in `[square]` and `(round)` brackets popping in as a pair, in violet, with a bracket later becoming a graphic | Punctuation can carry motion and structure a two-part claim |
| A wall of generated websites drifting diagonally | Volume shown at a glance proves capability |
| A closing bookend that returns to the bird on the branch, or to the opening horizon | Ending where you began makes a short film feel complete |

Also off-limits: the references' copy, music, footage, logos, color palettes as a set, and their exact beat structures.

## How to use this list

When you pitch concepts (see `launch-video`, step 2), check each one against the left column. If a concept's central image or its main transition is on this list, or is the same move in different clothes (a numeral made of product screenshots is still the numeral move), reject it and say why.

The right-hand column is free to use, because principles are not anyone's property. A bookend is fine; returning to the same kind of opening image the reference used is not.
