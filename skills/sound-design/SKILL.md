# Sound design

Sound is half the film. A film's sound is written by its own `films/<name>/score.py`, which `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/engine/render.mjs` runs while the frames render: it reads `build/manifest.json` (tempo, scenes, every cue the picture placed) and writes the mastered `build/mix.wav`.

## Where the music comes from

Claude can't hear and can't play. Earlier Slate scores were composed note by note and played on General MIDI samples (piano, Rhodes, strings); every one was judged synthetic, because a sampled acoustic instrument played from a script sounds like a sketch of a real player. So, in order:

1. **Compose it electronic, on `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/tools/synth.py`.** Synthesizers have no player to fall short of: a detuned saw pad, a filtered pluck, a sine sub and an 808-style kit *are* the real instruments when they're synthesized, and they are what most modern product and launch music is built from. Each film composes for its own cut, in code, like the picture (see Dayline's [`score.py`](../../films/dayline/score.py)).
2. **A track the person supplies** (licensed, or their own), when the brief wants a sound synthesis can't make (a band, a choir, a real piano). Fit it with `python3 ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/tools/music.py fit films/<name> music/their-track.mp3` and set `F.music({track})`.
3. **A track generated by Lyria** (Gemini API, needs `GEMINI_API_KEY`): `python3 ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/tools/music.py prompt|generate films/<name>`. The brief is written from the film's notes and timed outline. Ask the person to pick a take. Generated audio carries Google's SynthID watermark; never prompt for a named artist.

Never fall back to General MIDI instruments for a delivered film. `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/tools/score.py` (the old style scorer) remains only for quick drafts of films that have no `score.py` yet.

## Composing on synth.py

```python
from synth import Song
s = Song(bpm=120, beats=58)                          # the film's own tempo and length, from the manifest
s.bus('pad', verb=0.5, duck=0.45)                    # sends: hall reverb, ping-pong delay, sidechain from the kick
s.pad([57, 61, 64, 66, 69], at=0, beats=4, cutoff=900, open_to=1500)   # Dmaj9, filter opening across the bar
s.pluck(81, at=1, cutoff=600, env_amt=1500)          # a filtered two-saw pluck: closed is soft, open is bright
s.kick(at=16); s.clap(at=17); s.hat(at=16.5); s.sub(38, at=16, beats=1)
s.riser(at=26, beats=2); s.impact(at=48); s.bell(81, at=4)   # transitions and the one accent
s.click(t); s.tick(t); s.whoosh(t, 0.8)              # foley, at the picture's cue times (seconds)
s.master('build/mix.wav', lufs=-14, levels={'kick': -15, 'sub': -19, 'pad': -23, 'pluck': -19, ...})
```

- **Write the arrangement from the cut.** Sections start on the picture's own beats: the groove arrives with the press, drops out for a line of type, comes back fuller for the next proof, and resolves under the name. Read the cue times from the manifest rather than retyping them.
- **Harmony:** one chord per bar or two, voiced rootless in the middle register (the sub carries the root). Extended chords (maj9, m9, sus) sound produced; plain triads sound like a demo.
- **Arpeggios carry motion:** eighths when it's calm, sixteenths when the film is busy, a pattern that isn't a straight scale. Open the pluck's filter as the film opens up instead of adding instruments.
- **The mix is set by numbers:** `levels` is each bus's RMS *while it plays*, so a synth that came out 20 dB too quiet still sits where the mix says. Start from Dayline's levels.
- **Check without ears:** `--stems` writes each bus; plot a mel spectrogram next to a reference's and compare the band shares (below). Look for distinct notes in the mids, a low end that pulses with the kick, and air above 6 kHz only on hats and transitions.

## What produced launch music measures like

Measured from five professional launch films and ads (share of energy per band):

| Band | Sub <60 Hz | Low 60–150 | Low-mid 150–500 | Mid 500–2k | Presence 2–6k |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Range across the five | 10–60% | 4–33% | 14–35% | 10–36% | 2–5% |

- **There is always a sub.** At least a tenth of the energy sits below 60 Hz: an 808, a sub drone, a bass drum. Below 2% reads as thin, however good the notes are.
- **Tempo 89–136 BPM**, in a major key or a warm minor; integrated loudness −13 ± 1.5 LUFS.
- **The texture is a bed plus a lead**: a pad or section underneath, one recognisable lead on top (vibes, plucks, harp, piano), drums or pulse in the middle.
- **It keeps going.** None of the five stops dead before the logo; they swell, resolve and ring.

## The draft scorer (sketches only)

A film without its own `score.py` gets the old style scorer, `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/tools/score.py`, set with `F.music({key, style, arc, end})`. It is for timing drafts only: its sampled styles play General MIDI instruments, which every delivered Slate film so far was judged synthetic on, so never deliver on them. Compose on `synth.py` (above) for the final cut. The tables below describe what the draft scorer does. Tempo is the film's `bpm`.

### Sampled styles (drafts; need FluidSynth)

Played on sampled instruments through FluidSynth (`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/tools/arrange.py`, `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/tools/sampler.py`), with synthesized 808 kicks, subs, risers and booms, sidechained to the kick and balanced to the band shares above. They need `fluidsynth` and a General MIDI SoundFont (`apt install fluidsynth musescore-general-soundfont-lossless`, or set `SLATE_SOUNDFONT`); without one each falls back to its nearest sketch style and says so.

| Style | Ensemble | Moves by | For |
|---|---|---|---|
| `groove` | Warm pad, electric piano, 808 kick and sub, claps on 2 and 4, hats; landings on vibraphone | EP chords become swung stabs, hats go to sixteenths as energy rises | Everyday products, apps, ads with a pocket. 88–100 BPM |
| `pluck` | Harp and celesta arpeggios over a sub drone and a string section; timpani, then kick and shaker | Arpeggio goes from eighths to sixteenths; strings swell | Teasers, big reveals, wonder. 120–140 BPM |
| `cinema` | Strings, basses, oohs, celli pulsing in eighths, horns, deep drums on hits | The section swells, the pulse and horns enter | Brand films, stories, something serious. 70–100 BPM |
| `bright` | Strings, piano eighths, fingered bass, a full kit | Builds by layers, stays light | Optimistic consumer launches. 100–124 BPM |

### Sketch styles (synthesized)

Quieter, thinner, self-contained. Drafts, or the `room` style when the product's own sounds are the whole score.

| Style | Sound | Moves by | For |
|---|---|---|---|
| `solo` | One felt piano, rubato: a rolled left hand a hair late, a melody in breaths, a rest every fourth phrase | Touch and density; never adds instruments | Personal, considered, crafted products. 60–90 BPM |
| `strings` | A bowed chorale: cello on the root, three voices that move to the nearest note | The bow: it swells and recedes. No rhythm | Serious, premium, a story, a slow reveal. 60–80 BPM |
| `tape` | Electric piano comping on a swung grid, a round bass, brushes, all through worn tape | A groove in one gear; a kick and a walk-up appear when energy is up | Everyday, relaxed, consumer, call-and-response demos. 80–96 BPM |
| `wood` | Marimba ostinato accented 3+3+2, a low bar, a shaker when it lifts | Accents only when quiet, the full figure when up | Playful, light, handmade. 100–124 BPM |
| `felt` | Felt piano, a warm pad, a sub, brushes and a soft kick at high energy | Layers enter as energy rises | Calm, human products. 70–95 BPM |
| `glass` | Bell plucks over a bright pad | Layers enter as energy rises | Precise tools. 90–110 BPM |
| `pulse` | Minor pulse, kick on every beat, claps at the peak | Layers enter as energy rises | Trailers, ads that want energy. 115–135 BPM |
| `room` | No music. Foley and room tone only | The film's own actions | When the product's own sounds are the best score |

- **`gain`** sets the music against the foley (default 1). Lower it when the product's own sounds lead, so the film doesn't jump in level when the music enters.
- **`room`** raises the room tone (default 1) and adds air, for films where stretches have no music. Long stretches of near silence read as broken audio; a lived-in room reads as a choice.
- **The arc** (`[[t, energy], ...]`, 0–1) is the score's dynamics. It doesn't have to rise to the end: a flat arc is a steady score, a falling arc is a settle, a hump is a swell that recedes.
- **The ending** (`end`) is chosen per film. It plays from the `resolve` cue:

| `end` | What happens | Feels like |
|---|---|---|
| `ring` | A rolled chord on the style's own instrument rings out | Arrival, warmth |
| `phrase` | The music finishes its phrase on the cue; nothing added, the room rings | Understatement, confidence |
| `button` | The music stops on the cue with one short note in octaves | Wit, a full stop |
| `fade` | The music plays on under the end card and fades over `fadeLen` seconds (default 3) | It continues without you |
| `cut` | The music stops dead on the cue; only room tone after | Starkness, a held breath that isn't let out |

  Without a `resolve` cue the ending is always `fade`.
- **`drop`** stops the music dead until the next `thud` or `hit`, while the room keeps ringing. It is a trailer device: use it only when the form calls for it, and never as the default lead-in to the logo.
- **`land`** is foley (a soft paper tap). A note on every landing (mickey-mousing) makes the score a toy; when one rising run of landings really is the moment, set `F.music({landings: 'notes'})` and each `land` plays on the style's lead, its `i`/`n` picking the pitch. **`hit`** blooms on the style's own instruments.
- The mix gets a convolution room (larger for `solo` and `strings`), glue compression, a soft clip, and two-pass loudness normalization, then a true-peak limiter so sharp transients don't clip once encoded.

## Foley from the earshot kit (optional)

cinetic's sound kit plays designed effects with measured sync points: one soft sound per text fragment, a single clean element on the lockup, nothing stacked or rising. A Slate film can borrow its takes when earshot is installed (`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/docs/cinetic-setup.md`, Sound from earshot): render a kit with `python3 ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/cinetic/scripts/audio/kit.py render` in the film folder and mix the WAVs from `audio/kit/` at the cue times in `score.py`. The house restraint applies either way (`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/cinetic/references/sound.md` §5.7): near-silence while a line is read, one hit for the one reveal, and the lockup as a stop-down plus one element.

## Foley cues

| Cue | Sound | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| `rustle` (`len`) | paper crinkle and air | paper, fabric, many things moving |
| `land` | paper tap plus a piano note | anything settling into place |
| `thud` (`soft`) | low bloom plus a tap | something heavy arriving, the end of a drop |
| `swell` (`len`) | rising air | the build into a moment (sparingly) |
| `whoosh` (`len`) | panned air | fast moves across the frame |
| `flip` | a flick | cards and pages turning |
| `clap` | a clapperboard: two hardwood strikes and a dry crack | two things meeting hard on the beat |
| `click`, `key`, `tick`, `pop`, `glint` | UI clicks, keys, wood ticks, a bell sparkle | UI actions, small details |

Footage carries its own sounds: `F.cueClip(clip, at, {from})` puts every key press and click recorded by `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/engine/record.mjs` on the frame where it plays.

Put cues on the exact frame of the contact (the end of a landing tween, not its start). The score never guesses.

## Using a real track

1. `python3 ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/tools/beatgrid.py track.mp3` prints the BPM and the first downbeat.
2. Write the film in beats at a tempo close to the track's. `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/tools/music.py fit` stretches the track up to 8% to match the film exactly and puts its downbeat on beat 0, then `F.music({track: 'music/track.wav'})`.
3. Or render with `--music track.wav --offset <downbeat>` for a one-off. Foley is still generated and mixed on top.

Keep foley under a real track sparse: the product's own clicks and keys, and a few landings. A melodic note on every UI event (mickey-mousing) fights the music and sounds like a toy.

Use licensed or generated music only. Never pull a song from a video you were shown as a reference.

## Mastering targets

| Destination | Integrated | True peak |
|---|---|---|
| Web, YouTube, product pages (default) | −14 LUFS | −1 dBTP |
| Instagram, TikTok, X feeds | −13 to −12 LUFS | −1 dBTP |
| Presentation playback | −16 LUFS | −1 dBTP |

## Checking without ears

You can't listen, so look and measure:
- `ffmpeg -i build/mix.wav -lavfi showspectrumpic=s=1600x500:scale=log:fscale=log spec.png` shows the layers, the drop (a vertical gap), the landings and the tail.
- The critic's loudness curve should have the shape you chose: steady for a take, falling for a settle, a hump for a swell.
- Compare the band shares with the table above (`python3 ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/tools/critic.py` prints them). Under 2% below 60 Hz is thin; over 70% below 150 Hz is muddy.
- Tell the person plainly that you checked the sound by measurement and that they should listen.
